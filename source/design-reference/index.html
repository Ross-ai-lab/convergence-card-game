Card stat symbols — how other games make it obvious

How card games signal COST, ATTACK and HEALTH. The question this answers: which number is which, without reading a label. Shapes here are original SVG reconstructions of the CONVENTION (a crystal, a sword, a drop) — not copies of any game's artwork. Source links are for checking the facts, not for copying assets.

Hearthstone

The reference point for this whole convention. Three different shapes AND three different colours — nothing relies on position alone.

source: https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Card

Legends of Runeterra

Cost top-left in a gem. Power and health sit at the bottom corners in the same left/right order as Hearthstone — that left=offence, right=defence split is the strongest cross-game convention there is.

source: https://support-legendsofruneterra.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035562134-Card-Types-and-How-They-Work

Magic: The Gathering (Arena)

The outlier, and a useful warning: mana pips are top-right, and power/toughness share ONE box bottom-right written as 3/4. It works because players learn the slash, not because it is self-explanatory. Do not copy this.

source: https://magic.wizards.com/en/rules

Convergence (this game, current)

Built 2026-07-30. Shape AND colour, never position alone. Health is a heart because the player's own core is a heart too. The attack sword took three tries: a wide hexagonal blade read as a house, a blade-over-pommel read as a coin with a cross on it, and only a DOWNWARD sword — grip, wide crossguard, blade tapering to a point — reads as a weapon at card size. The number sits in the blade.

What to take from it

  • Colour is read before shape, and shape before position. Encode meaning in at least two of the three; three identical discs told apart only by corner is the failure this file exists to prevent.
  • Blue = cost, red = health and warm gold = attack are near-universal. Runeterra's green shield for health is the one common variation, and it still pairs a distinct colour with a distinct shape.
  • Bottom-left = offence, bottom-right = defence holds across every game here. Keep it.
  • A container shape must be WIDER than the number it holds. The first sword here had a 40-unit blade against a ~40-unit digit, so the number sat above the shape instead of inside it and read as two separate marks. Check that at full size, not on a thumbnail.
  • A weapon icon only reads if the crossguard is WIDE and the blade TAPERS. Symmetrical blocky shapes collapse into a house or a shield at small sizes, whatever the intent. Point the sword down: it puts the widest part of the blade where the number has to go.
