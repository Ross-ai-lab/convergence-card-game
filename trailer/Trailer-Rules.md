# Convergence trailer rules

## Purpose

Create an epic, cinematic, fast-paced trailer for friends. Show actual gameplay and collecting characters. Emphasize the game's cross-Universe combinations, Relics, voiced champions and rewards.

## Finished output

- Deliver one full-quality MP4, approximately 90 seconds long.
- Use 1920 by 1080 pixels and 60 frames per second.
- Record genuine new frames at 60 fps. Do not raise the fps by repeating frames or interpolating old footage.
- Remove the separate sharing trailer and matching poster. Do not regenerate either.
- Do not publish the trailer or send it to friends automatically.

## Content and wording

- Use “Universes” instead of “worlds” in every trailer title and spoken promotional line.
- Use “Conquer Universes. Claim their characters.” for the reward section.
- Use “Use iconic Relics to power your minions” for the Relic section.
- Show a more exciting Relic than Green Lantern Ring. Demonstrate Infinity Stone's effect through the real combat engine.
- Replace the “four slots” selling point with a stronger distinction: unexpected allies and cross-Universe synergies.
- Remove the online section, website link and link-based ending.
- Remove small yellow titles wherever a white headline already states the message.
- Prefer one clear headline per scene. Avoid redundant explanatory text.

## Cards and footage

- Give each card or featured character one continuous appearance in the trailer. Do not bring it back in a later scene.
- Never place two copies of the same card type on a shown board.
- Keep a cast ledger that assigns every card to one scene. Check it before every build.
- A continuous gameplay appearance may include equipping, inspection and combat. Do not insert a separate repeat montage of those cards.
- Goku, Darth Vader and Godzilla appear in the opening only.
- Use new characters in the ending montage.
- Remove accidental hover previews, including the enlarged Military Fort.
- Decode full-resolution card art before capture. Do not record blurred loading previews.
- Keep important card art, effects, names and gameplay readable. Avoid excessive zooming and cropping.
- Use isolated saves. Stage through existing development hooks, then perform real engine actions.
- Do not alter game balance, card rules or the owner's progress to make a shot.

## Sound

- Keep “Your command is acknowledged” in the opening.
- Use additional existing character voice lines throughout the trailer.
- Match each character's voice to its appearance.
- Keep dialogue clear above the score. Duck music during speech.
- Keep complete phrases. Never cut off a word to meet a scene duration.
- Build a score with changes in intensity, impacts and a resolved ending.

## Repeatable production and verification

- Keep the capture fix in the shared video pipeline and explain it in the existing video knowledge page.
- Build through the existing video front door. Keep project source in the trailer folder.
- Record capture fps, frame count, distinct-frame measurements, actions and cast checks.
- Check frame count and actual motion, not only the output file's fps label.
- Verify the final duration, picture, audio, complete decoding, titles, cast ledger and removed outputs.
- Inspect a representative frame from every scene and moving sections with capture concerns.
- Report listening limitations honestly. A loudness measurement is not a listening check.

This source is copied to the Windows Desktop by the preparation script, so the desktop rules remain current.
